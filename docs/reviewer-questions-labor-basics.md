# Labor Basics: Questions for a Clinical Reviewer

**Prepared:** October 2026, from the first-pass [source check](source-check-2026-10.md).
**For:** a clinician reviewing Module 2, "Labor Basics and When to Call", before any lesson is marked `reviewed`.

The source fixes that needed no clinical judgment have already been made: supporting pages were added and moved ACOG links were updated. They're listed at the end. Everything below needs a clinician's decision about the wording, or a source only a person can open.

Slide numbers count from 1, including the title slide, as they play in the class. The slide id in brackets matches the lesson file.

## Safety slides (please review first)

### 1. Pushing with an epidural: rest first, or push right away?
**Lesson 5, slide 2** (`what-it-feels-like`)
- **What the slide says:** with an epidural, the care team "may suggest resting for a while to let your baby move down" before pushing (delayed pushing).
- **What we found:** since 2018, ACOG has said it is reasonable to choose *immediate* pushing for first-time parents with an epidural. We could not read CPG No. 8's full text to confirm what it says now.
- **Question:** keep, reword (for example, "your team will guide when to start pushing; some suggest resting first, others pushing right away"), or remove?

### 2. Unplanned birth before reaching care has no real source
**Lesson 5, slide 12** (`fast-birth`, a safety slide)
- **What the slide says:** call 911, pull over if driving, don't pull on the cord, keep the baby skin to skin and warm.
- **What we found:** its only source is the AAP 2022 breastfeeding policy, which doesn't cover emergency birth and couldn't be opened automatically. A partial option is the Lamaze article "How to Safely Deliver a Baby When Medical Assistance is Still Minutes Away" (2014), which supports not pulling the cord and keeping the baby warm skin to skin, but not "call 911" or "pull over".
- **Question:** which source should back this slide? Is any of the wording beyond what a source supports?

### 3. "Call 911" on the warning-sign slides
**Lesson 1, slide 13** (`urgent-warning-signs`) · **Lesson 2, slides 8 and 9** (`bleeding`, `urgent-warning-signs`) · **Lesson 3, slide 12** (`call-any-stage`) · **Lesson 4, slide 11** (`call-now`) · **Lesson 6, slide 11** (`heavy-bleeding`)
- **What we found:** the cited CDC Hear Her page lists the symptoms but says "Seek medical care immediately", not "call 911". No cited source says 911.
- **Question:** keep "call 911" as BabyReady's own safety guidance, note it as editorial, or find a source?

### 4. Preterm labor signs: "spotting"
**Lesson 1, slide 12** (`preterm-signs`)
- **Done:** added NICHD's "What are the symptoms of preterm labor?" page, which supports "contractions every 10 minutes or more often".
- **Still open:** that page lists "leaking fluid or bleeding from the vagina" but doesn't say "spotting" by name. The slide says "change in vaginal discharge, spotting, or bleeding".
- **Question:** keep "spotting" (stricter, and consistent with ACNM's advice to call even for spotting), or match NICHD's wording?

### 5. Bleeding in pregnancy: "even spotting"
**Lesson 2, slide 8** (`bleeding`)
- **What we found:** CDC's wording for pregnancy is bleeding "more than spotting". The slide says to call even for spotting, which is backed by ACNM (also cited).
- **Question:** confirm the stricter advice is intended.

## Teaching slides

### 6. Active labor at 6 cm, and stage definitions
**Lesson 3, slides 2–10 and 15** (cite ACOG CPG No. 8)
- **What we found:** only CPG 8's summary is on acog.org. The full text is on the journal site (marked "Free Access"), which blocked our automated reader. Secondary summaries say CPG 8 puts active labor at 6 cm.
- **Question:** please confirm 6 cm and the stage descriptions against the full text. Transition symptoms, "I can't do this", and effacement terms (slides 3 and 7) are probably not in a clinical guideline. Is a patient-education source needed for those?

### 7. Early labor at home (ACOG Committee Opinion 766)
**Lesson 4, slides 2, 4 and 6** (`why-home`, `eat-drink`, `comfort-tools`)
- **What we found:** the summary of CO 766 (reaffirmed 2025) supports self-care during latent labor at home and nonpharmacologic coping. It doesn't confirm that "admission can usually wait" (slide 2 attributes this to ACOG), eating and drinking (slide 4), or showers and baths after the water breaks (slide 6).
- **Done:** added WHO 2018 (recommendation 24: food and fluids during labor for low-risk women) to slide 4.
- **Question:** confirm these against the full text. The Lamaze source on slide 4 is a 2015 advocacy blog post. Keep it, or drop it now that WHO is cited?

### 8. Pushing approaches and how long pushing takes
**Lesson 5, slides 4 and 6** (`how-to-push`, `how-long`)
- **What we found:** we couldn't confirm "both approaches work" for urge-guided and coached pushing, or that pushing often takes longer with a first baby or an epidural, from the summaries alone.
- **Question:** confirm against the CPG 8 and CO 766 full texts.

### 9. Tears and repair (ACOG Practice Bulletin 198)
**Lesson 5, slides 7, 8 and 14; Lesson 6, slide 9**
- **What we found:** PB 198's full text is members-only. The free AAFP article "Obstetric Lacerations" (2021) supports the same claims and is now cited on all these slides.
- **Question:** keep PB 198 as a second source if a reviewer can open it, or rely on AAFP?

### 10. Newborn after birth (AAP 2022 breastfeeding policy)
**Lesson 5, slide 10** (`baby-is-born`) · **Lesson 6, slides 6, 7, 8, 10 and 14**
- **What we found:** the AAP page blocks automated readers, so a person needs to open it. WHO 2018 (recommendations 48–50) is now cited alongside it for skin-to-skin, early feeding, and vitamin K after the first hour.
- **Still unsupported by either page we could read:**
  - skin-to-skin steadying "breathing, heart rate, and blood sugar" (Lesson 6, slide 6)
  - feeding cues such as rooting and licking (slide 7)
  - bluish color and white coating at birth (Lesson 5, slide 10)
- **Question:** confirm these in the AAP policy, or soften the wording.

### 11. Apgar score details
**Lesson 6, slide 8** (`baby-checks`)
- **What we found:** the summary of ACOG Committee Opinion 644 confirms the Apgar score reports the baby's status right after birth. It doesn't confirm the 1- and 5-minute timing, the five things checked, or that it is "often done on your chest".
- **Question:** confirm against the full text, or add a patient-education source.

### 12. Smaller unsupported details
- **Lesson 1, slide 2:** "37–38 weeks = early term" and "due date set at 40 weeks" aren't on the NICHD page. Note that ACOG FAQ004 says most births happen between 38 and 41 weeks, while the slide uses NICHD's 37–42. Is an ACOG source needed for "early term"?
- **Lesson 1, slides 8 and 10:**
  - How to time contractions (start to start, start to end) isn't on a cited page.
  - "Note the time and what the fluid looks like" isn't on a cited page either.
- **Lesson 2:**
  - "Brown" fluid (slide 6) isn't on a cited page.
  - "Babies don't run out of room" (slide 7) isn't on a cited page.
  - The "what to have ready when you call" list (slide 10) isn't on a cited page.
- **Lesson 3, slide 11:** support tips for each stage aren't on a cited page.
- **Lesson 4, slide 7:** "time a few contractions in a row, not every one" isn't on a cited page.
- **Lesson 6:**
  - "Placenta usually within about half an hour" (slides 2 and 14) isn't on a cited page.
  - Shaking or chills after birth (slide 9) isn't on a cited page.
  - "Some midwives wait even longer" to clamp (slide 4) isn't on a cited page.
  - ACOG CO 814 also mentions a small increase in jaundice with delayed clamping. Should the slide mention it?
- **Question:** for each one, find a source, mark it as editorial, or trim the wording.
